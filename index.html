The problem

A brochure plan gives you sizes. You need to know how it feels.

The questions that decide a purchase are hard to answer from a flat drawing.

Will the living room get sun in the evening?Orientation and window position are in the drawing, but the light is not.
Will our bed and wardrobe fit?Room sizes are printed as feet and inches, which is hard to picture.
Which type, and which facing?A project can have a dozen plans that look alike on paper.

What it does

See it, light it. Furnishing is next.

The real sun, in every room

Pick a date and a time in Hyderabad. Light and shadows follow the actual position of the sun, and Homewalk estimates the hours of direct sun each room gets and when.

  • Date and time scrubber, with sunrise and sunset for that day
  • Direct-sun hours and time window for every room
  • Orientation taken from the brochure's own compass

Tour it in 3D

Orbit the whole flat, switch to an iso or top view, cut the walls away to see inside, or walk through it at eye level. Doors open.

  • Orbit, iso, top, cutaway and walk modes
  • Room labels with areas
  • Fly to any room from the room strip
In progress

Furnish it and check the fit

Being built. An early version already lets you drag furniture from a library of 60 items onto the plan, rotate it, measure walls, and see an estimate of the interiors and flooring cost in rupees.

  • Dimensioned 2D plan with room areas
  • Furniture shown in every plan
  • Placing your own furniture: in progress
